# PMI San Diego 40th Anniversary Exhibit

## Retrospective project plan

This document reconstructs how I organized and delivered the work, using my project experience, meeting notes, shared-document feedback, and the event planner. It was prepared after completion.

### Project responsibilities

My primary assignment was to research and design the historical exhibit for PMI San Diego’s 40th Anniversary. The event program, scavenger-hunt materials, and gift vouchers were added during the project.

I organized my work around research, content development, visual design, stakeholder review, revisions, and final preparation for the September 12, 2026 event.

### 1. Establish the research direction

My supervisor, Robyn, provided talking points, research ideas, reference materials, and websites to explore. These established the initial direction for the exhibit.

I reviewed those materials and researched related events to expand the chapter’s story and connect its development with San Diego’s wider historical context.

### 2. Develop and check the content

I turned the research into a written exhibit script and organized it into a chronological timeline.

Before moving into visual design, I checked information against available sources to assess its accuracy and avoid unsupported claims. Gaps in the chapter’s historical records required particular care when selecting names, dates, and leadership details.

The script provided the foundation for deciding which information belonged together and how to present it across the exhibit.

### 3. Design the historical exhibit

I organized the content into 10 posters measuring 24 × 36 inches. Dividing the research into historical periods helped manage overlapping information and create a clear sequence for attendees.

Because the celebration took place in the evening and lighting was a concern, I prioritized readable content, clear visual hierarchy, and layouts that attendees could browse comfortably.

The supplied PMI branding guidelines informed the presentation of text, images, logos, and other visual elements.

### 4. Review and revise with stakeholders

I shared research and designs incrementally and met with Robyn to discuss revisions. These discussions led to adjustments in content, wording, and image selection.

I tracked feedback through meeting notes and comments on shared documents, then incorporated requested changes into subsequent versions. Robyn reviewed and approved my materials.

One significant decision was to emphasize chapter milestones and collective contributions rather than individual presidents and historical leaders. Some early leaders were no longer living, and incomplete records made it difficult to confirm details and represent everyone fairly. Focusing on the chapter’s shared history reduced the risk of unintentionally excluding contributors.

### 5. Integrate additional deliverables

The program, scavenger-hunt materials, and gift vouchers expanded my workload beyond the historical exhibit.

After the exhibit, I prioritized the program and scavenger hunt because of their importance to the attendee experience:

* The program communicated the event schedule and sequence of activities.
* The scavenger hunt supported one of the event’s main networking activities.
* The gift vouchers supported the event’s professional-development prizes.

I considered both event importance and deadlines when deciding what to work on next.

### 6. Adjust the schedule

The additional tasks caused delays, so I reassessed priorities and adjusted my work schedule. I contributed additional volunteer hours to complete the expanded workload and prevent further delays.

Meeting notes and shared-document comments helped me track outstanding revisions as I worked toward the final deadline.
