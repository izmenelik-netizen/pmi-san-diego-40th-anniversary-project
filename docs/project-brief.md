# PMI San Diego 40th Anniversary Exhibit

## Retrospective project brief

Prepared after project completion using project materials, the event planner, research documents, and my account of the work.

### Purpose

The historical exhibit was created to celebrate PMI San Diego’s growth and the people who helped shape its project management community over four decades.

The exhibit introduced the broader PMI and California chapter background before tracing San Diego’s history from its establishment in 1986 to its 40th anniversary in 2026. It connected chapter milestones, volunteer contributions, and leadership stories with the changing industries and professional environment of San Diego.

For longtime members, the exhibit was intended to provide a meaningful walk down memory lane, recognizing experiences and contributions they had lived through. For newer members and other attendees, it offered an accessible introduction to how the chapter developed and why its history matters.

### Objectives

* Research the chapter’s origins, development, leadership, and significant milestones.
* Transform historical information into a visually appealing and understandable exhibit.
* Organize overlapping research into a clear chronological narrative.
* Recognize the contributions of chapter members, volunteers, and leaders.
* Design materials that attendees could comfortably browse during an evening event.
* Incorporate stakeholder feedback and prepare approved materials within the event schedule.

### Scope and deliverables

My primary responsibility was researching and designing the historical exhibit. The final exhibit document contains 10 posters in a 24 × 36-inch format, beginning with a historical prologue and continuing through the chapter’s anniversary.

My work included selecting and organizing historical content, developing poster copy, creating layouts, incorporating imagery, and revising materials following review.

Supporting event materials included:

* A two-page event program with sponsor recognition and a survey QR code.
* A zoo scavenger-hunt handout.
* A “What’s your PM spirit animal?” visual.
* Gift vouchers for professional-development prizes.

The broader event team coordinated event logistics. My contribution focused on historical research, visual communication, exhibit development, and supporting event materials.

### My role and design decisions

**Role: Event Exhibit Designer — volunteer**

I researched the chapter's history and translated that information into visual storytelling for the anniversary celebration. I developed layouts, timelines, graphics, and supporting materials while coordinating with my supervisor and event team.

I chose to organize the historical exhibit into 10 posters because much of the research overlapped. Dividing the material into distinct periods helped create a manageable sequence and reduce repetition.

Because the event took place in the evening and lighting was a concern, I prioritized ease of viewing and a clear visual hierarchy. The intent was to help attendees identify each poster’s main theme and understand the story while moving through a busy event environment.

### Constraints and requirements

**Fixed deadlines:** Research, design, review, and production had to fit the event schedule, including the September 9 printing milestone and September 12 event deadline. Revisions needed to leave sufficient time for final preparation.

**Historical accuracy:** Research needed to be grounded in the supplied chapter notes, historical research, poster outline, and relevant source websites. Dates, names, and leadership claims required careful checking. The important-figures research explicitly marks some proposed founding leaders as unverified; those entries should not be treated as confirmed without additional evidence.

**Brand requirements:** The supplied PMI Global Marketing Guidelines established requirements for brand presentation, including sentence-case headlines, appropriate logo use, consistent margins, a 10-column layout grid, and authentic photography.

**Evening viewing conditions:** The exhibit needed to communicate clearly in an evening venue with limited lighting. Readability, visual hierarchy, and manageable amounts of text were therefore important design considerations.

**Physical format and content capacity:** The historical exhibit used 10 posters measuring 24 × 36 inches. Four decades of chapter history, plus introductory context, had to fit within that space while remaining easy to follow.

**Stakeholder approval:** Research and designs needed to accommodate review, requested edits, and approval from my supervisor before release.
