# PMI San Diego 40th Anniversary Exhibit

## Results and lessons learned

This retrospective combines my account of project delivery with the September 16, 2026 planning committee debrief and its accompanying worksheet. Committee findings describe the wider event; personal reflections address my research, design, and coordination responsibilities.

### Delivery results

I completed all materials by September 9, 2026, ahead of the September 12 anniversary event.

My deliverables included:

* A historical exhibit comprising 10 posters in a 24 × 36-inch format.
* An event program with sponsor recognition and a survey QR code.
* Scavenger-hunt materials.
* A project-management animal-persona visual.
* Professional-development gift vouchers.

The program, scavenger hunt, and vouchers were added during development. This expanded workload caused delays, which I addressed by revising my schedule, prioritizing essential materials, and contributing additional volunteer hours.

### Event-level results

The committee debriefs recorded 87 attendees against a target of 200. Of 93 registered guests, six did not attend.

Ten guests responded to the event survey. Those respondents rated the event 4.5 out of 5 overall and reported an average likelihood of returning of 8.9 out of 10.

These figures describe the overall event and a small survey sample. They do not measure the historical exhibit’s individual impact.

### What worked well

**Iterative review:** Sharing work incrementally and discussing revisions with Robyn allowed me to refine content, wording, and images throughout development.

**Documented feedback:** Meeting notes and comments on shared documents helped me track requested changes and incorporate them into later versions.

**Prioritization:** Reassessing the importance and deadlines of added deliverables helped me complete the expanded assignment before the event.

**Team flexibility:** The committee identified volunteers’ willingness to pitch in, regular meetings, and a dedicated team as strengths.

**Scavenger-hunt concept:** The committee recommended continuing the scavenger hunt, while improving how participants were introduced to it.

### Lessons and future improvements

| Lesson                                                  | Evidence or experience                                                                                    | What I would do next time                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Added scope needs an explicit schedule review.          | Additional deliverables caused delays and required extra volunteer hours.                                 | Review effort, priorities, dependencies, and available support with my supervisor when new tasks are added.                         |
| Historical research needs traceable sources.            | Incomplete chapter records complicated decisions about historical figures and recognition.                | Maintain a source register that distinguishes confirmed information from claims requiring verification.                             |
| An exhibit needs visibility within the event program.   | The committee recommended drawing more attention to the history exhibit.                                  | Coordinate exhibit placement, signage, and an announcement or invitation to view it with the event team.                            |
| Activity design includes participant communication.     | The worksheet noted that the scavenger hunt was not communicated well at check-in.                        | Provide clear instructions, brief check-in volunteers, and include a start time in the program even when participation is optional. |
| Materials must be considered alongside setup logistics. | The committee identified preparation of easels and other setup items as an improvement area.              | Confirm display equipment, quantities, placement, and setup responsibilities with the logistics team in advance.                    |
| Rehearsals should include supporting materials.         | The committee recommended a fuller run-through of the exhibit, raffles, remarks, and program transitions. | Review how each material will be introduced and used during an event walkthrough.                                                   |
| Flexible teamwork benefits from clear ownership.        | The committee valued hands-on support but requested more specific assignments and additional volunteers.  | Agree on named owners for review, production, setup, and attendee guidance.                                                         |

### Recommendations from the committee

The debrief recommended a longer planning runway, with detailed planning beginning six to nine months before future events and an announcement approximately one year ahead.

Other recommendations included dedicated marketing and communications roles, earlier graphics, more guided networking, clearer event-day responsibilities, and advance preparation of setup materials.

The worksheet records follow-up actions as proposed and not started. These are recommendations for future work, rather than completed improvements.

### Personal takeaway

This project strengthened my ability to turn research into a visual narrative, incorporate stakeholder feedback, and manage competing deliverables against a fixed event date.

My main lesson is to assess the schedule impact of additional work earlier and coordinate how materials will be used at the venue as carefully as how they are designed. Completing the files is one part of delivery; helping attendees find, understand, and use them is equally important.
